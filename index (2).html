1 First-year priorities

Understand, assess, prioritise, embed

Maturity isn’t whether a policy exists. It’s whether leaders own it, people follow it and controls work.

  1. 0–90 daysUnderstand
  2. 3–6 monthsAssess and prioritise
  3. 6–12 monthsBuild and embed
  • Meet the team and key leaders across Risk, Data Strategy, Claims, Technology, Legal and InfoSec
  • Map data ecosystems and the change pipeline
  • Surface urgent risks and quick wins
  • Baseline against the ICO Accountability Framework
  • Test key controls, not just documents
  • Separate urgent remediation from the longer-term roadmap
  • Strengthen DPIA, DSAR, AI and data-governance controls
  • Board MI and KRIs live

3 Enabling innovation and change

Make the safe route the easy route

Early engagement, simple governance and proportionate triage add up to faster, safer change.

  1. TriageLow, medium or high risk, so effort goes where it matters
  2. Assess onceOne route covering DPIA, AI and data-sharing questions
  3. DesignMinimise, secure, explain, keep human oversight
  4. DecideMitigate, accept within appetite, or escalate
  5. MonitorControl drift, incidents, lessons learned

Applied to MIB’s 2026 agenda. Select one to see the privacy focus.

Focus: accuracy, purpose limits and a full audit trail, so a council never refuses a licence on stale or wrong data.

Focus: lineage, security and clear sharing agreements with every insurer sending data in real time.

Focus: health data under Article 9, Article 22 safeguards, meaningful human review and fairness testing.

Focus: necessity, minimisation and clear controllership before any manufacturer data flows.

Focus: transparent profiling, PECR compliance, and extra care where campaigns reach young people.

My default question isn’t “can we?” It’s “what’s the safest proportionate way to achieve this?”

4 Risk management and regulators

Make bad news travel fast

A speak-up culture gives MIB time to contain harm, make defensible decisions and engage transparently.

Breaches

Early escalation, a risk-of-harm assessment, a documented 72-hour ICO decision and root-cause fixes.

Complex DSARs

Triage and scope early, apply exemptions such as crime and fraud prevention carefully, and quality-assure redaction.

Investigations

A single point of contact, evidence packs, Board visibility and remediation tracked to closure.

Consistent response playbook

  1. Triage
  2. Contain
  3. Decide
  4. Notify
  5. Learn

Priority risk register: sensitive and criminal-offence data, profiling and automation, data quality and access, sharing and third parties.

A constructive ICO relationship

No surprises
Early contact on material issues.
Evidence-led
Explain our judgement and show remediation.
Forward-looking
Engage early on AI and connected-vehicle questions.
Joined-up
Align with DfT, police partners and members where data issues cross over.
5 Leadership and culture

Trusted partner, independent voice

Privacy culture is strongest when people involve the DPO because they value the advice, not because a process forces them to.

Privacy culture

  • Leadership tone set from ExCo
  • Privacy champions in every directorate
  • Role-based training for claims, data teams and executives
  • Early escalation feels safe

High-performing function

  • Clear domain ownership across the team
  • Senior people coach and grow others
  • Regular feedback, not annual surprises
  • Capability built beyond the team, so the DPO isn’t the bottleneck

Independence in practice

  • Direct access to the Board and Risk Committee
  • Challenge documented and escalated when needed
  • No conflicting operational ownership
  • Close enough to understand, independent enough to challenge
